○ A card reaches friendship training at gauge 80, which the client draws as the fourth mark and colours orange. The gauge rises about +5 per joint training, so a card with no head start needs most of a run to get there — which is why the "Initial Friendship Gauge" effect is the one that matters.

Annotation key · Support deck

  1. Gauge state is a word and a bar, not a colour. "Gauge 80 · orange" prints the value and the stage the client uses; the bar is decoration and is aria-hidden. The client's own gauge turns orange at the fourth mark, so the label matches what a Trainer sees in game.
  2. Pal cards say what they do not do. "No friendship bonus" is printed on the card rather than discovered mid-run. The export is unambiguous: zero of the 11 JP SSR Pal cards carry the friendship effect, and all 296 other SSRs do.
  3. The multiplier is stated as multiplication. 1.25 × 1.30 = 1.625, with the arithmetic shown. Stating "+55%" would teach the wrong model, and the wrong model costs a run.
  4. Diagnostics are checks with pass and caution states, not a score. There is no deck rating, because no source publishes one — and the advisor pattern in this app never invents a number it cannot source.
  5. Duplicate cards are prevented by the client, mirrored here as a check. Two identical copies cannot sit in one deck; duplicates are spent on limit breaks instead. The card's own four-break diamonds are not modelled on this screen because level caps are a per-card fact, not a deck fact.
  6. Miller’s Law bounds what is held, not what is shown. The deck is six slots because the client caps it at six, so the screen never asks a Trainer to weigh more than six cards at once. The pool behind it is filtered rather than scrolled, which keeps the choice count inside the same limit.
  7. Heuristic coverage. H6. Six slots with the type of each, so the deck is recognised as a shape rather than recalled as a list.
  8. Framework coverage. Norman on constraints, and Tognazzini 9 on human-interface objects. The picker offers no card that is already in the deck, so the client rule is expressed by the control instead of by a message after the fact. The remove control carries its visible glyph inside its accessible name, so a voice-control user can say what they see.
  10. The rail collapses, and the word never leaves the DOM. The app can collapse its global navigation to marks instead of words, and this set does the same (AppLayout.vue:191-233). A collapsed destination moves its label to sr-only and gains a title, so it keeps its accessible name; the active one keeps aria-current="page" and a left rule, so state never rides on colour alone. The control is at the head of the rail, where DESIGN.md §2.2 puts a control that belongs to a pane: the penalty for a distant control is exactly why the top-bar version was wrong. The choice is localStorage, not a Preference row, because Preference::KEYS is exactly theme and failure_estimate.
  12. The type is a chip, and the chip is never the mark alone. The client identifies a support card by the chip it wears, so the type column carries the client's own glyphs in the client's own chip colours. The word stays beside the mark, because a mark alone breaks WCAG 1.4.1 and because a stat carries no saturated identity hue (DESIGN-CORPUS.md:371-393): the identity is the glyph plus a whisper of band tint, and the hue is spent on meaning.
  13. The picture slots are drawings, and absence is a normal state. The app renders a mirrored support_thumb beside each card in a slot at 48px (SupportSlot.vue, DESIGN.md §4.7). A file the mirror does not hold renders nothing — no grey box, no placeholder glyph — and the slot keeps its name. An empty slot carries no frame at all, because there is no card to picture. The dashed frames here mark cells the picture would occupy, and the subject inside each names the picture that belongs there.
