Annotation key · Event decision

  1. Options are choices, not outcomes. Every option says "effect not published" rather than carrying a guessed benefit. The app's own rule is that it never invents a score or a probability (ADR-0001 §3, ADR-0020 §2), and an event is exactly where that rule is easiest to break.
  2. The selected option is marked three ways — radio, pick border, tint — consistent with every other selection surface in the wireframe.
  3. Outcome recording is a separate block. The app records what the client showed, after the fact. It does not pre-fill an expected result, because a forecast printed on the choice screen would become advice nobody gave.
  4. Retryability is stated up front. "Alarm Clock covers goal races only" answers the one question a Trainer has after a bad event, before they look for an undo that does not exist.
  5. Event copy is not paraphrased. The title here is a labelled placeholder. Real client copy is printed verbatim or not at all — paraphrasing game text is how a tracker starts teaching the wrong event.
  6. Hick’s Law bounds the choice count. The client presents a vertical stack of one to three options, so the screen designs for three and never for a scrollable list. Every option states that its effect is unpublished rather than carrying an estimate, which keeps three real options rather than three guesses.
  7. Heuristic coverage. H5. Options are choices with their unpublished effect stated, not outcomes with a guessed benefit, which prevents a wrong commit rather than reporting it.
  8. Framework coverage. Information architecture 2 on choices, and Shneiderman 5 on error prevention. The choice list is sized to the client's one to three options, and each option says its effect is unpublished rather than carrying a guess that would prevent the error by being wrong.
