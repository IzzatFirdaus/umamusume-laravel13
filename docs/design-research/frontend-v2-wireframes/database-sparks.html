The inheritance loop

StepWhat happens
1Train to a final total.
2Bank the factor grade into a veteran record.
3Inherit the +16 per ★3 at the next run's start.
4Train past the previous ceiling.

○ The loop repeats at every inheritance moment. Because blue sparks raise the ceiling rather than a starting value, the payoff compounds across a run instead of arriving once.

Annotation key · Sparks

  1. Two families get full value tables; two get a sentence. Blue and pink have published per-grade figures that decide a build; white and unique do not, so they are listed without invented numbers.
  2. "Per two parents" is computed, not typed. The column exists because the common decision is whether to run two of the same family, and doubling is the arithmetic.
  3. Entered versus published ceiling is restated here too. It is the same distinction the cockpit audit filed as a defect, and a reference page is the cheapest place to teach it.
  4. Pareto Principle splits the two families that matter from the two that do not. Blue and pink carry published per-grade figures that decide an inheritance, so they get full value tables; green and white get a sentence, because their numbers are not published and a table of blanks implies otherwise.
  5. Heuristic coverage. H6. The two families with published figures get tables and the two without get a sentence, so the reader knows which numbers exist.
  6. Framework coverage. Gerhardt-Powals 7 on limiting data-driven tasks and Information architecture 6 on multiple classification. The two families with published figures get tables and the two without get a sentence, and a spark is reachable by family, grade and owner.
  8. The rail collapses, and the word never leaves the DOM. The app can collapse its global navigation to marks instead of words, and this set does the same (AppLayout.vue:191-233). A collapsed destination moves its label to sr-only and gains a title, so it keeps its accessible name; the active one keeps aria-current="page" and a left rule, so state never rides on colour alone. The control is in the top bar because the bar is the one row present at every width. The choice is localStorage, not a Preference row, because Preference::KEYS is exactly theme and failure_estimate.
  10. The way back to the hub is a strip, not a word in a corner. The breadcrumb linked "Database", but it sits in the top bar on the brand-blue chrome with no underline, so it read as a word rather than as a link and a Trainer looking for the way back found the global rail instead. The nine areas are one family, so they are grouped as one family: a sub-navigation strip of the nine, with the current one marked, and the breadcrumb link now visibly a link. Information Architecture principle 7 (focused navigation) and principle 5 (front doors).
