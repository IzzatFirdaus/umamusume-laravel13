Annotation key · Skills planner

  1. Base, discount and cost are three columns, not one. A Trainer deciding between skills needs to see why 130 costs 91; showing only the final price hides the value of earning a hint.
  2. The budget bar is the Goal-Gradient. Remaining 33 is printed as a number and set apart in green, so the constraint is visible while browsing rather than discovered at commit.
  3. Unaffordable rows stay visible and say why. "Too expensive" rather than a hidden row. Same rule as the race screen's blocked aptitude row.
  4. Planning is not learning. Checkboxes build a plan; a single "Learn planned skills" commits it. That separates browsing from a write, which is the preview-before-commit pattern the corpus declares (DESIGN-CORPUS.md §5).
  5. The ladder is explained because its shape is surprising. Most players assume a flat 10% per level; the client says otherwise past Lv3. Printing the table once is cheaper than a Trainer mis-budgeting a skill.
  6. Heuristic coverage. H1. SP remaining, hint level and the projected cost all move together, so the effect of a pick is visible before it is taken.
  7. Framework coverage. Gerhardt-Powals 3 on fusing data, and Information architecture 6 on multiple classification. SP, hint level and the projected cost move together, and the library is reachable by category, by distance and by running style.

Wireframe only. The hint ladder, the two worked price samples and the 1,910 skill count are sourced (UMAMUSUME_REFERENCE.md §1.1.4; live dashboard count). Skill names and the SP balance are illustrative.
