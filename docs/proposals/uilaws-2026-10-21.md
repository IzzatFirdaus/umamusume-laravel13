## The thirteen, verbatim

### 1. Symmetry
> The human eye tends to perceive symmetrical elements as a single, unified whole.

### 2. Rule of Thirds
> Dividing a layout into a 3x3 grid and placing key elements along these lines creates more balanced designs.

### 3. White Space
> Proper use of white space enhances readability, focus, and overall visual appeal.

### 4. Color Theory
> Colors evoke emotions and associations, and their combinations can create harmony or discord in a design.

### 5. Typography Hierarchy
> A clear hierarchy in text sizes and styles guides users through content and improves readability.

### 6. Consistency
> Maintaining consistency in design elements across an interface enhances usability and aesthetic appeal.

### 7. Proximity
> Elements that are close to each other tend to be perceived as related or grouped together.

### 8. Contrast
> Elements that stand out from their surroundings are more likely to grab attention and be remembered.

### 9. Closure
> The human mind tends to perceive complete shapes even when parts of the shape are missing.

### 10. Continuity
> Elements arranged in a line or curve are perceived as more related than elements not on the line or curve.

### 11. Fitts's Law
> The time to acquire a target is a function of the distance to and size of the target.

### 12. Hick's Law
> The time it takes to make a decision increases with the number and complexity of choices.

### 13. Jakob's Law
> Users spend most of their time on other sites, so they prefer your site to work the same way as all the other sites they already know.

## What each decides in this repository

| Law | The decision it settles |
| --- | --- |
| **Symmetry (1)** | Where content is genuinely paired, the pair is laid out symmetrically. Where it is not, the asymmetry is honest: a three-quarters plus one-quarter split is not a violation. |
| **Rule of Thirds (2)** | **Deliberately not applied.** A three-by-three grid is a composition rule for a marketing surface. Forcing a stat table into thirds would be ornament, and the repository's density is a judgement. |
| **White Space (3)** | The gutters carry the grouping. A card's padding is generous because the space is what separates one idea from the next, not because it looks airy. |
| **Color Theory (4)** | Every hue in the system is semantic: green is action, orange is increase, blue is decrease, cyan is Skill Points, gold is selected, crimson is risk, indigo is rank. That is why the five stats carry **no** identity hue (`DESIGN-CORPUS.md:371-393`): a hue per stat would spend the palette on meaninglessness. |
| **Typography Hierarchy (5)** | Three steps, and only three: the page title, the section heading, the body. The shipped app's 75 headers at `text-base` is the defect this avoids. |
| **Consistency (6)** | One token set, one type scale, one focus ring, one component behaviour per role. |
| **Proximity (7)** | A label inside its own `<label>`; a provenance mark beside the number; a state chip beside its word. |
| **Contrast (8)** | The one accented element per screen, and **never colour alone**: every state carries a word or a glyph. |
| **Closure (9)** | **Permitted visually, forbidden informationally.** A partial indicator may look complete, but it must not claim completeness in its text. An empty ancestor slot reads "slot open", not a closed-up box. |
| **Continuity (10)** | The timeline is a rail and the rail never breaks. The inheritance diagram draws the connection between generations. |
| **Fitts (11)** | 44px minimum. |
| **Hick (12)** | The option count is bounded on every decision surface. |
| **Jakob (13)** | A conventional sidebar, table, form and stepper. |
